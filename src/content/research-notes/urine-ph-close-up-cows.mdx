---
title: "What Urine pH Really Tells Us in Close-Up Cows"
date: "2026-08-28"
---

Urine pH is the verification step for a DCAD program, not a standalone target to formulate toward. It reflects the cow's actual acid-base status in response to what she's eating right now — not what the ration calculates to on paper.

For Holsteins on a properly acidified close-up diet, target urine pH generally runs 6.0–6.8. Jerseys typically run a touch lower. Readings persistently above 7.0 despite a calculated negative DCAD usually mean one of three things: the forage mineral analysis used to build the ration is out of date, anion product intake is inconsistent (check sorting), or water mineral content — sodium and bicarbonate in particular — is offsetting the acidifying effect.

Sample mid-day, from a representative 10–15% of the close-up group, 5–7 days into the diet. A single early sample tells you less than a pattern across a few days.
