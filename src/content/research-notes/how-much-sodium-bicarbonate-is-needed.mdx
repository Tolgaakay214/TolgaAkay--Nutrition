---
title: "How Much Sodium Bicarbonate Is Actually Needed?"
date: "2026-07-31"
---

Sodium bicarbonate is one of the most reached-for rumen buffers, and one of the most inconsistently dosed. Typical inclusion in lactating diets runs roughly 0.5–1.5% of ration DM, but the right number depends entirely on the fermentability of the rest of the diet — a ration already well-supplied with physically effective fiber and moderate starch needs far less buffering support than a high-starch, aggressively fermentable ration.

Bicarbonate is a support tool for buffering capacity, not a substitute for adequate effective fiber. Reaching for more bicarbonate to compensate for a ration that's fundamentally short on structural fiber treats a symptom rather than the cause, and rarely resolves milk fat depression on its own.
