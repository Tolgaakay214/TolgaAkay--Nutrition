---
title: "Why Starch Digestibility Changes After Ensiling"
date: "2026-08-14"
---

Corn silage starch digestibility increases measurably with time in storage — a well-documented effect driven by proteolytic breakdown of the zein protein matrix that surrounds starch granules in the kernel. As storage time increases (particularly through 3–6 months and beyond), that protein matrix breaks down, exposing more starch to rumen microbial attack.

The practical implication: freshly ensiled corn silage and the same silage six months later are not nutritionally identical, even with unchanged lab starch content. Rations formulated tightly at chop-out may need starch or fermentability adjustments as the pile ages, particularly for herds sensitive to SARA risk.
