---
title: "Why Cows Sort Against Long Particles"
date: "2026-08-21"
---

Sorting isn't random pickiness — it's a cow optimizing for the most energy-dense, least effortful bite available, which is a rational response to how a TMR is presented. Long, dry, or poorly incorporated forage particles are harder to prehend and chew relative to fine, moist concentrate particles, so given the choice, cows preferentially consume the fine fraction first.

The two biggest levers you actually control are TMR moisture and mix uniformity. Rations under roughly 45% moisture sort more; getting closer to 50–55% (with water or a wet by-product) measurably reduces sorting in most herds. Over-mixing matters too — it can shred forage particles down into the sortable fine fraction just as effectively as starting with forage chopped too short.
